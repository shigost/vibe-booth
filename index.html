Gear guide how this maps to real booths

Decks are media players

Club booths use a pair of professional media players. Each plays one track, and the big jog wheel nudges, scratches, and cues. At home most people learn on an all-in-one DJ controller connected to DJ software.

The mixer and its channels

The middle of the booth is the DJ mixer. Each channel has a 3-band EQ (Hi, Mid, and Low), a filter knob, and a volume fader, which is exactly what each channel in Mix has here.

Crossfader

Slides between the two sides of the mixer. House and techno DJs often blend slowly with channel faders and EQ, while hip hop and scratch DJs use a sharp crossfader. Both work here.

EQ and the bass swap

Two kicks and two basslines at once turn to mud. Pros cut Low on the incoming track, blend it in, then swap: cut Low on the old track and bring Low back on the new one, right on a phrase change.

Song structure and phrasing

Every track now plays like a real DJ track: an 8-bar intro of drums with the filter opening, a groove, an 8-bar build with a riser, a snare roll, and a one-beat pause, then a 16-bar drop, a breakdown with pads and vocals, a second build and drop, and a drums-and-bass outro. DJs mix in during intros and out during outros, and line up drops on the first beat of a phrase. Each deck shows which section it is in and which bar.

Hot cues, loops, and rolls

Hot cues are saved spots in a track: tap Intro, Build, Drop, or Break to jump there on the next bar. Loop 4 repeats the current 4 bars so you can stretch a groove while you set up the other deck. Holding Roll repeats a short slice of the beat for a stutter, while the track keeps moving underneath, the way slip mode works on real players. Phaser sweeps the whole mix, and Gate chops it into a rhythm.

Island sounds

Under the music you can hear the island: rolling surf, foam, shell chimes on the breeze, and lava crackling (louder when the volcano erupts). The Island kit uses rattling shells for hi-hats, a woody log hit for the snare, and a bass inspired by the belembaotuyan, the CHamoru musical bow with a gourd resonator, whose tone players change by moving the gourd. Every drop is announced with a kulo' call, the triton shell trumpet.

BPM by genre

House 120 to 128, tech house 124 to 128, techno 128 to 140, trance 136 to 140, dubstep 140 (half time), future bass 140 to 160, and drum and bass 170 to 176.

The Studio grid is a drum machine

It works like a classic step-sequencer drum machine: 16 steps make one bar of 4 beats. In music production software this is the same as drawing a one-bar MIDI clip, so patterns you make here carry straight into your own sessions.

The island and its details

The stage is a guma' latte, a house raised on latte stones: haligi pillars topped with tasa capstones, the foundation of CHamoru homes in the Latte period. The lasers fire from kulo' (triton shell trumpets), a sinahi crescent of shell hangs at the peak of the roof, shell-bead garlands line the rafters, and a lusong (stone mortar) and red clay pots sit near the house. Out on the water, a sakman, the fast outrigger sailing canoe Europeans called the flying proa, circles the island with a haggan (sea turtle). Its sail doubles as a screen: Puntan and Fu'una, whose creation story turned Puntan's eyes into the sun and moon and his eyebrows into rainbows; pictographs in the spirit of the cave paintings at Gadao's Cave and Litekyan; and latte stones beneath a sinahi moon.

Sound kits

Garage kits use shuffly 2-step drums (that is what Swing does), warm piano chords, deep subs, and chopped vocal "oohs" that duck under the kick. Riddim kits use half-time drums at 150 bpm and a wobble bass whose filter is swept by an LFO synced to the tempo. Alt pop kits use crunchy guitars and bouncy indie beats. Every kit uses sidechain, where the kick briefly turns the bass and chords down so the groove pumps.

Show design

Festival stages run lasers, CO2, pyro flame cannons, and LED walls, usually programmed by a lighting designer on a lighting console or timecoded to the set. VJs run visuals live with video software. The Show tab is your mini version of that.

How to play

Tap a deck. Slide to blend. Build, then drop.

Tap a record, or the Play button under it, to start a deck. It always waits for the next bar, so it lands on the beat. Shigost poofs over to the decks and plays along.

Slide the crossfader toward A or B to choose which deck the crowd hears. In the middle, you hear both.

Tap Drop for a big moment. More controls (EQ, pads, effects, Build, Flames, and Lasers) appear as you finish quests, or turn on Show all controls.

Hover over or long-press any control to see what it does.

Tap things on the island: Shigost, the volcano, the lava vents, the round screen, the water, the palms, the haggan, and the sakman. Drag to look around; pinch or scroll to zoom.

Quests in Learn teach real DJ moves, one at a time.

FAQ

I can't hear anything. Check that your volume is up. On a phone, tap anywhere on the page to wake the sound if it stopped. Headphones sound best.

Where did some controls go? New players start with the essentials, and more controls appear as you finish quests. Turn on Show all controls in Mix to see everything now. On a computer, Full, Compact, and Hide above the decks change how much of the controller shows.

Does it save my progress? Yes, in this browser on this device: your quests, saved tracks, and settings. Clearing your browser's site data resets them.

The lights are too much. In Show, turn on Reduce flashing lights. Comfort mode steadies the camera in Shigost's view and the Reef view.

Can I play on my phone? Yes, upright or on its side. On its side you hold it like a controller, with the rest of the controls behind the ☰ button.

Something looks broken. On a computer, press F to show performance details you can share with us. On a phone, add ?debug to the end of the address.

CHamoru culture in Vibe Booth

Vibe Booth is set on a volcanic island in the Marianas and draws on CHamoru culture with care. The stage is a guma' latte, a house raised on latte stones: haligi pillars topped with tasa capstones. You'll also find a sinahi crescent, the call of a kulo' (a triton shell trumpet), a sakman sailing canoe, and island trees like nunu (banyan), dokdok (seeded breadfruit), and pågo (sea hibiscus). Håfa adai means hello.

These are shared as a celebration, not a reconstruction. If something is wrong or could be better, please let us know.
